D-Daybook Privacy Policy

For the D-Daybook app on iPhone and Apple Watch. Last updated 8 November 2026.

The short version

  • D-Daybook has no servers of its own. Your calendar, reminders, lists and notes stay on your iPhone and in your own iCloud. We cannot see them.
  • No accounts with us, no adverts, no analytics and no tracking. We do not sell or share your data.

Who we are

D-Daybook is made by Dave U in the United Kingdom. For any privacy question, email ddaydeveloper.app@gmail.com.

What D-Daybook uses, and why

Calendar and Reminders

With your permission, D-Daybook reads and changes your Apple Calendar and Apple Reminders to show your day, your lists and your shopping. These stay in Apple's apps. Apple syncs them through iCloud if you have that switched on. D-Daybook does not copy them anywhere else.

Places for new events

When you type a place for a new event, D-Daybook asks Apple Maps for matching places. What you type goes to Apple for that search, under Apple's privacy policy. The place you pick is saved with the event in your calendar.

Location, for the weather

If you allow it, D-Daybook uses your location while the app is open to get today's weather from Apple Weather. Your location goes only to Apple, in that request. D-Daybook keeps the last weather it received, not your location. You can switch location off in the iPhone Settings app at any time.

Location, for Notify me when I arrive at a shop

The setting Notify me when I arrive at a shop only works for shopping items you tag with a shop (for example Lidl or Aldi), and you can switch them off in D-Daybook's Settings. D-Daybook asks Apple Maps where the stores of those shops are near you, using your approximate location, under Apple's privacy policy. Your iPhone then watches for you arriving at one of them, even when the app is closed, and shows one alert with the items you tagged for that shop. Your location and your shop tags stay on your phone and are never sent to us or anyone else. Switch the alerts off in D-Daybook's Settings, or location off in the iPhone Settings app, at any time.

Camera and photos, for receipts and shopping items

If you scan a paper receipt, D-Daybook uses the camera (or a photo you choose) only for that. The receipt is read on your phone by Apple's built-in text reading. The picture is kept on your phone, so you can look at it again or show it in a shop, and is never sent anywhere unless you share it yourself. D-Daybook keeps the shop name, date, total and each item with its price in a small file on your phone, to tick off your shopping list, show what you spent and show what things usually cost at each shop. You can delete any scanned receipt, with its picture, in Shopping, Spending. You can also put a photo on a shopping item (taken with the camera or chosen from your photos); it is shrunk and kept on your phone; only a short link to it is added to the item in Apple Reminders. If you invite people to share the photos on a shopping list, that list's photos are also kept in an iCloud space that only those people can see (Apple's iCloud sharing; D-Daybook has no server). Lists with nobody invited keep their photos on your phone. If you scan a product's barcode to add it, the barcode is read on your phone and only the barcode number is sent to Open Food Facts (a free, open product database) to find the product's name.

Handwritten notes

Notes you write or type in D-Daybook, with Apple Pencil, a finger or the keyboard, are kept on your phone and in the iCloud copy described below. Your handwriting is read on your phone only, by Apple's built-in handwriting recognition, so you can search your notes, so a note can take its name from its first line, and so you can turn writing into typed text. The words it finds, and anything you type, are kept with the note. Nothing you write is sent to us or to anyone else unless you share it yourself, for example as a PDF. Deleted notes stay in Recently deleted for 30 days, then go for good, or sooner if you delete them from there.

iCloud and Apple Watch

To keep the Apple Watch up to date when your iPhone is far away, D-Daybook saves a summary of your day and lists in your own private iCloud storage. Ticks and items you add on the watch go back the same way. The summary is end-to-end encrypted, and only your devices can read it. We cannot see it. When your watch is near your iPhone, the two talk to each other directly.

So that you keep your information when you change phones, D-Daybook also saves a copy of what it stores in its own files to your own private iCloud storage: handwritten notes, scanned receipts and their pictures, item photos, shop tags, categories, buying habits, and your settings. Only your Apple ID can reach this copy, and we cannot see it. A new phone signed in to the same Apple ID gets it back when you open D-Daybook. You can turn this off in D-Daybook, under Settings, iCloud.

Comparing shopping prices

In the UK, the Compare prices button copies your shopping list to your iPhone's clipboard and opens the Pennypea website. D-Daybook sends nothing to Pennypea itself. If you paste your list there, Pennypea's own privacy policy applies.

Sending us feedback

Send feedback, in D-Daybook's Settings, opens an email to us in your own mail app. It adds the app's version, your iPhone model and your iOS version, to help fix problems. You see every word, and nothing is sent unless you tap Send. We use your email only to reply to you and improve the app, and delete it when it is no longer needed.

Notifications and Siri

Alerts are created on your iPhone. When you use Siri with D-Daybook, Apple handles your voice under Apple's privacy policy, and D-Daybook only receives the words needed to add the item.

What we do not collect

D-Daybook has no sign-up and no account with us. It contains no advertising, analytics or tracking code, and no third-party software kits. Nothing you do in the app is sent to us.

Other companies involved

  • Apple provides Calendar, Reminders, iCloud, Apple Maps, Apple Weather, and Siri, under Apple's privacy policy.
  • When you open Pennypea, or a product is looked up on Open Food Facts, that service works in your browser, under its own policy.

Keeping and deleting your data

Scanned receipts and notes stay until you delete them. Everything D-Daybook stores is on your devices or in your iCloud. To delete it all, delete the app. To clear the iCloud copy too, open the iPhone Settings app, then your name at the top, then iCloud, then Storage, and delete D-Daybook's data.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask what data is held about you, and ask for it to be corrected or deleted. We hold none on our own systems, but we are happy to help: email ddaydeveloper.app@gmail.com. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office.

Children

D-Daybook is not designed for children under 13 and does not knowingly collect any data from them.

Changes to this policy

If D-Daybook changes how it handles data, for example by adding a server for instant alerts, we will update this page and say so in the app before the change takes effect.
