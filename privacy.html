D-Daybook Privacy Policy

For the D-Daybook app on iPhone and Apple Watch. Last updated 28 October 2026.

The short version

  • D-Daybook has no servers of its own. Your calendar, reminders and emails stay on your iPhone, in your own iCloud, or with the service they came from.
  • If you connect Gmail, your emails are read on your iPhone by Apple's on-device AI. They are never sent to us or to any AI company.
  • No accounts with us, no adverts, no analytics and no tracking. We do not sell or share your data.

Who we are

D-Daybook is made by Dave U, a sole trader in the United Kingdom, trading as D-Daybook. For any privacy question, email ddaydeveloper.app@gmail.com.

What D-Daybook uses, and why

Calendar and Reminders

With your permission, D-Daybook reads and changes your Apple Calendar and Apple Reminders to show your day, your lists and your shopping. These stay in Apple's apps. Apple syncs them through iCloud if you have that switched on. D-Daybook does not copy them anywhere else.

Places for new events

When you type a place for a new event, D-Daybook asks Apple Maps for matching places. What you type goes to Apple for that search, under Apple's privacy policy. The place you pick is saved with the event in your calendar.

Gmail (optional)

You can connect one or more Gmail accounts to find mail tasks, deliveries, receipts and subscriptions. D-Daybook asks Google for read-only access, so it can never send, delete or change your email.

  • Emails go straight from Google to your iPhone. They are read there, by Apple's on-device AI model.
  • D-Daybook keeps a small file on your iPhone with what it found: short titles, shop names, amounts, dates, parcel status, email IDs and the choices you made (such as "not a receipt"). It does not store email subjects or message text. These are fetched when you open an email and are only held while you look at them.
  • Your Google sign-in key is kept in the iPhone's secure Keychain.
  • Signing out of an account removes its sign-in key. Deleting the app removes everything D-Daybook stored.

Location, for the weather

If you allow it, D-Daybook uses your location while the app is open to get today's weather from Apple Weather. Your location goes only to Apple, in that request. D-Daybook keeps the last weather it received, not your location. You can switch location off in the iPhone Settings app at any time.

iCloud and Apple Watch

To keep the Apple Watch up to date when your iPhone is far away, D-Daybook saves a summary of your day and lists in your own private iCloud storage. Ticks and items you add on the watch go back the same way. The summary is end-to-end encrypted, and only your devices can read it. We cannot see it. When your watch is near your iPhone, the two talk to each other directly.

Comparing shopping prices

In the UK, the Compare prices button copies your shopping list to your iPhone's clipboard and opens the Pennypea website. D-Daybook sends nothing to Pennypea itself. If you paste your list there, Pennypea's own privacy policy applies.

Notifications and Siri

Alerts are created on your iPhone. When you use Siri with D-Daybook, Apple handles your voice under Apple's privacy policy, and D-Daybook only receives the words needed to add the item.

What we do not collect

D-Daybook has no sign-up and no account with us. It contains no advertising, analytics or tracking code, and no third-party software kits. Nothing you do in the app is sent to us.

Other companies involved

  • Apple provides Calendar, Reminders, iCloud, Apple Maps, Apple Weather, Siri and the on-device AI model, under Apple's privacy policy.
  • Google provides Gmail, under Google's privacy policy. You can remove D-Daybook's access at any time in your Google account settings.
  • When you tap a parcel tracking link, or open Pennypea, that website opens in your browser, under its own policy.

Google user data

D-Daybook's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Gmail data is used only to show you features in D-Daybook. It is not transferred to anyone, not used for advertising, not used to train AI models, and not read by people.

Keeping and deleting your data

D-Daybook keeps found receipts for up to a year and drops old parcels after a week without news. Everything D-Daybook stores is on your devices or in your iCloud. To delete it all, delete the app. To clear the iCloud copy too, go to Settings, then your name, then iCloud, then Manage Account Storage, and remove D-Daybook.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask what data is held about you, and ask for it to be corrected or deleted. We hold none on our own systems, but we are happy to help: email ddaydeveloper.app@gmail.com. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office.

Children

D-Daybook is not designed for children under 13 and does not knowingly collect any data from them.

Changes to this policy

If D-Daybook changes how it handles data, for example by adding a server for instant alerts, we will update this page and say so in the app before the change takes effect.
